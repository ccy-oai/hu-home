# Echoes of the Ember Suns
## Part I of the *Suns of the Exile* Trilogy

> *"The galaxy is not saved by heroes. It is saved by the choices of ordinary hearts when no one is watching."*  
> — Inscription found on the shattered gates of Karth

---

## Prologue — The Night the Stars Went Quiet

The first star went dark over the mining moon of Veyra-9.

No flare. No warning. It simply folded in on itself like a fist closing around a candle.

In the watching tower, a young signal-warden named Ral Nesh pressed both palms to the cold glass of the observation dome. Beyond it hung the black scaffold of the tethered shipyards, their lights blinking in precise sequences that had governed his life since childhood. Haulers came in. Refiners bled ore. Convoys left under escort of the Protectorate.

Tonight, the blink-patterns stuttered.

Ral's console chimed with a priority broadcast. The seal of the Protectorate burned in red across the screen: a stylized sun pierced by a vertical blade.

"All stations," the voice said, smooth as polished metal. "Remain calm. A localized stellar anomaly has been detected in the Veyra system. The Protectorate Fleet is responding. All civilians are ordered to shelter in hardened facilities until further notice. This is a lawful directive." 

Ral had never heard the Fleet speak of anomalies. Anomalies were the language of old myths, the sort his grandmother whispered when power outages swept the hab-blocks and the moon's thin wind keened along the alloy ribs.

He opened the external feeds.

Ships were already moving.

Not the slow, stately migrations of ore cruisers, but knife-fast war frigates that sliced across the starfield in serried lines. Their wakes painted pale scars in the dark. In the upper yards, automated clamps released an unfinished battleship—the *Judgment of Dawn*—before its hull plates were fully sealed.

Veyra-9 had always been a place where the galaxy took and never gave back. The moon scraped its people into the shape of work. But even here, there had been a kind of rhythm, an agreement between exhaustion and survival.

The rhythm broke when the second star died.

This time, the tower shook.

Every alarm rang at once. Heat signatures cascaded in red across Ral's displays as the sky itself seemed to tilt. Far out in the system, the tether of the primary solar mirror snapped like thread. Its vast petals spun loose, glittering and useless.

Ral keyed in a desperate request for clarification.

No reply came from Fleet Command.

Instead, a new signal pierced every channel, narrow and bright, like a blade of sound driven straight into the heart of the system.

It carried no seal and no origin marker, only a repeating line in an ancient trade-tongue few still studied:

**THE EMBER SUNS ARE OPEN.**

Ral did not understand it.

He did understand the faces of the dockworkers on the lower feeds, turned upward and lit by the glow of collapsing light.

He did understand what it meant when the Protectorate ordered the yards scuttled and the atmosphere shields lowered without explanation.

In the time it took for the third star to falter, Veyra-9 learned the first lesson of the coming war:

The galaxy could be silenced.

## Chapter 1 — A Thief in the Pilgrim's Market

On the pilgrim-world of Ashael, the stars never went quiet.

They sang.

Wind chimes hung from every arch. Prism lanterns scattered shards of color across the white stone streets. Pilgrims came to Ashael from a thousand systems to stand beneath the Singing Spires and hear the harmonic resonance of the planet's crystalline mantle. Some called it sacred. Others called it geology dressed in silk.

Tamsin Vale called it cover.

She slipped through the crowd like a note too soft to follow, hood low, satchel tight against her side. Her target stood near the fountain of Returning Light: a Protectorate courier with a blue-gold sash, flanked by two armored sentries.

Couriers were always sentries of a different kind. They carried messages small enough to lose and important enough to kill for.

Tamsin had no interest in killing.

She had a very keen interest in losing messages.

She brushed a pilgrim's shoulder, apologized in three languages, and used the motion to angle into the courier's blind side. Her fingers found the seam of the courier case, slipped in a wafer of flexwire, and lifted.

The latch whispered open.

Inside, stacked like prayer tablets, were six data slivers.

She took four.

Greed was for amateurs. Precision was for survivors.

She sealed the case, stepped away, and was halfway to the market colonnade when the sky shuddered.

The Singing Spires keened.

Every crystal on Ashael—lantern, chime, spire—vibrated on a single, terrible note that cut through the air like a blade drawn slowly from its sheath.

People stopped. Faces lifted. Tamsin felt the sound in her teeth.

A flare of light split the midday sky, not from the sun but from the space above it, a white wound tearing itself open between layers of atmosphere.

Ships fell through.

Not descended—fell, as if gravity had decided that space was no longer optional.

The Protectorate courier shouted into his wristcom. The sentries raised rifles. Pilgrims screamed and ran, the market breaking apart into streams of cloth and panic.

Tamsin ducked beneath a spice vendor's awning and thumbed one of the stolen slivers into her reader.

The file was protected by military cipher. She bypassed it with a stolen keyring embedded in her wristjack and skimmed the header.

**PROJECT: EMBER SUNS**  
**AUTHORITY: LORD-COMMANDER SERIX THANE**  
**CLASSIFICATION: ABSOLUTE**

The rest of the file resolved in jagged blocks as the reader strained to keep up. A diagram unfolded: concentric rings around a star, a lattice of dark nodes, and beneath it a single line of annotation.

**WEAPONIZED STELLAR SUPPRESSION ARRAY — PROOF OF CONCEPT COMPLETE**

Tamsin stared at the words while the heavens cracked overhead.

On instinct, she ran.

## Chapter 2 — The Last Student of the Hollow Order

Joren Kael hated the desert, but the desert loved him.

On the rim-world of Karth, wind carved canyons into bone-colored stone, and the sun burned everything down to its essential truths. You could not lie to Karth. You could only endure it.

Joren endured it barefoot atop the training mesa, eyes closed, palms open to the heat.

"Again," said Master Ilyra.

She stood in the shade of a shattered arch, robes the color of stormglass, a staff of polished iron resting across her shoulders. Around her throat hung a ring of black crystal—the sign of the Hollow Order, a forgotten lineage of guardians who studied the Resonance: a field of subtle energy that flowed through living minds, binding perception, intention, and probability in ways the old Republic once revered and the Protectorate now outlawed.

Joren breathed in the air like fire and reached for the Resonance.

It came to him as it always did: not as a power but as a listening. The creak of cooling stone. The scratch of a dune lizard beneath the sand. The precise moment a gust would change direction and carry grit across his skin.

He nudged that moment.

The wind bent.

A spiral of dust lifted around him, slow and deliberate as a dancer turning.

"Better," Ilyra said. "Now hold it." 

Joren held it until sweat ran into his eyes and the world narrowed to the drumbeat of his pulse. A second gust crashed into the first and tore the spiral apart. Sand slapped his face.

He opened his eyes, coughing.

Ilyra smiled without kindness.

"Control is not a cage," she said. "It is a conversation. And you are still shouting." 

Before Joren could reply, the comm-beacon mounted in the arch sputtered to life. It had been dead for months, its power cells scavenged, its circuits a nest for beetles. Now it flickered with a pale, stubborn light.

A voice crawled out of it, warped by distance.

"...any Hollow survivors... repeat... the Protectorate has deployed the Ember Suns... stars are—"

Static swallowed the rest.

Joren and Ilyra looked at each other.

The Ember Suns were a story told only in the oldest of the Order's hidden vaults: a rumored catastrophe device designed at the end of the Republic's civil wars, a weapon so abhorrent its schematics were supposedly sealed behind a vow of silence and the self-destruction of entire research worlds.

"That is not possible," Joren said.

Ilyra's gaze went to the sky, where the sun hung untroubled and cruel.

"Possible," she said softly, "is what men decide when they believe they are the last ones who matter." 

She turned, staff tapping once against stone.

"Pack the vault. We leave at nightfall." 

"Leave?" Joren's voice cracked with disbelief. "Master, Karth is hidden. The Protectorate won't—"

A shadow passed over the mesa.

He looked up.

It was not a cloud.

Three wedge-shaped destroyers drifted across the sun like the closing of a lid.

## Chapter 3 — The Archivist and the Lie

Senator Maera Voss had built her career on the careful arrangement of truths.

In the Chamber of Systems, beneath a dome of projected stars, she stood at her console while the Protectorate's High Marshal delivered his address.

"The Ember Suns initiative," Marshal Corven said, voice amplified into every seat and every screen across the core worlds, "is a defensive measure. It allows us to dampen unstable stellar activity in insurgent regions, preventing catastrophic solar events and safeguarding billions. It is the culmination of decades of sacrifice and brilliant science. Those who oppose it oppose order itself." 

The Chamber erupted in rehearsed applause.

Maera did not clap.

She watched the projection above the Marshal: a simulation of a star gently dimming, its light folding into orderly bands like silk drawn through a ring. It was beautiful. It was also a lie.

Two hours earlier, she had received a ghost packet routed through six relay monasteries and a derelict weather station. It contained a single image captured from the Veyra system: the dark husk of a star, its corona gone, its planets frozen mid-orbit like beads on a broken string.

A caption had been embedded beneath the image.

**SILENCED. NOT STABILIZED.**

Maera's aide leaned close. "Senator, the vote is in twelve minutes. The Marshal expects your endorsement."

Of course he did.

Maera Voss was the Protectorate's favorite reformist—sharp enough to be useful, pragmatic enough to be trusted, principled enough to be paraded. She had played the role for years because it allowed her to nudge policy away from cruelty in ways small enough to survive scrutiny.

This was not small.

She opened her private channel and summoned the only person she trusted to tell her when she was being a fool.

The holoplate shimmered, resolving into the lined face of Archivist Pell Orun, keeper of the Deep Stacks beneath the Senate.

"Pell," she said without preamble. "Tell me about the Ember Suns." 

The old man's eyes darkened.

"I was hoping you would never ask," he said.

"You archived the Republic's war records. You know what it is." 

"I know what it was meant to be," Pell said. "A theoretical deterrent against starforge arrays during the Schism Wars. The research was buried. The scientists were sworn to silence under oath and sealed into cryovaults that were supposed to drift forever." 

"Supposed to?" 

"Three years ago, the Protectorate granted a salvage license to Lord-Commander Serix Thane." Pell's voice thinned with disgust. "They called it historical recovery. He called it ambition." 

The Chamber lights dimmed in preparation for the vote. A hush settled like dust.

"If I oppose this," Maera said quietly, "I will be removed. Replaced by someone who will sign anything they put in front of them." 

"If you endorse it," Pell said, "you will help extinguish suns." 

Maera closed her eyes.

When she opened them, she had made a decision that would unmake the life she had built.

"Find me a way out," she said. "And find me someone who can break Thane's weapon." 

Pell's expression shifted, not quite hope, not quite fear.

"There are rumors," he said. "Of a Hollow adept on Karth. Of a thief on Ashael who steals from couriers and gives to rebels. Of old networks waking up."

Maera allowed herself a thin, dangerous smile.

"Then let us become rumors," she said.

## Chapter 4 — Ashfall on Karth

The destroyers did not bother with subtlety.

They opened their bays and let drop-ships rain down the canyon like iron seeds. Each ship struck the sand with a thunderclap and unfolded into ranks of white-armored troopers whose visors burned with scanning light.

Joren and Ilyra watched from the vault mouth carved into the cliffside, their packs heavy with crystal holocrons and half a century of forbidden history.

"We cannot fight a legion," Joren said.

"We are not fighting a legion," Ilyra said. "We are escaping one." 

She pressed her palm to the vault door. The Resonance thrummed under the stone, ancient and sorrowful. The door sealed behind them with a groan like a beast settling into sleep.

They descended into the canyon's labyrinth, keeping to shadow. Twice, patrols passed close enough for Joren to hear the whisper of servomotors. Each time, Ilyra stilled the air around them, bending sight and sound just enough that the troopers' eyes slid away.

It should have worked.

It almost did.

At the canyon's throat, they found their path blocked by a single figure in black armor, helm crested with a vertical fin. A crimson blade of coherent light extended from the hilt in his hand with a hiss like steam on hot stone.

Joren felt the Resonance recoil.

"Master Ilyra," the figure said, voice filtered but unmistakably human. "By order of Lord-Commander Thane, you are to surrender all artifacts and submit for relocation." 

"Relocation," Ilyra said, and laughed once. "A gentle word for erasure." 

The inquisitor tilted his head. "You are a relic of a dangerous time. The Protectorate offers you mercy."

"The Protectorate offers oblivion," Ilyra said. "We decline." 

She moved.

Joren had seen her spar a hundred times, but never like this. Her staff met the crimson blade in a burst of white sparks as she pivoted inside the inquisitor's guard. She struck his helm, his knee, his wrist, each blow guided by the Resonance's subtle tug. The inquisitor staggered, then recovered with terrifying speed.

The duel became a storm.

Joren wanted to help. Ilyra had forbidden it. "You are not ready," she had said. He hated that she was right.

A trooper squad rounded the bend behind them, rifles rising.

Joren chose.

He stepped into the open and flung both hands toward the canyon wall.

The Resonance answered like a chorus suddenly given a conductor. Stone groaned. A slab the size of a shuttle sheared loose and crashed down between them and the troopers, burying the squad in a bloom of dust and screaming metal.

The effort buckled Joren's knees. He tasted blood.

The inquisitor saw the opening. His blade pierced Ilyra's side beneath her ribs.

Time stuttered.

Ilyra did not fall immediately. She turned her head toward Joren, eyes bright with pain and an iron clarity that terrified him more than the wound.

"Run," she said.

"No." 

"Joren." Her voice cracked like the vault door. "Listen to me. The vault is not the Order. You are. Run." 

She struck the inquisitor one last time, staff slamming into his helm hard enough to crack the crest. Then she did something Joren had never seen: she opened herself fully to the Resonance.

It roared.

Wind exploded down the canyon in a howling wall that tore sand from stone and flung the inquisitor backward into the cliff. Joren felt the force lift him, carry him, hurl him into the dark of a side passage.

When he staggered to his feet, coughing, the canyon behind him was a blur of dust, light, and distant shouting.

He ran until the sun was a memory and the night swallowed Karth whole.

## Chapter 5 — The Courier Who Didn't Die

Tamsin expected to be dead by sundown.

Stealing from the Protectorate was survivable. Stealing *during* a planetary incursion was not. Yet as the sky burned and ships screamed overhead, she found herself ducking into the undercroft tunnels beneath Ashael's market district, very much alive and very much not alone.

A boy in courier blue crouched beside a locked maintenance door, hands shaking as he tried to override the panel. His sash was gone. Blood ran from a cut along his temple.

Tamsin recognized him instantly.

The courier she'd robbed.

He looked up, saw her, and flinched. "You," he spat. "Thief." 

"Accurate," Tamsin said. She knelt, pulled a thin spike from her sleeve, and jammed it into the panel seam. "Move." 

"Why would I—"

The tunnel shook. Dust sifted down. Somewhere above, a spire shattered with a sound like a bell being torn in half.

The boy moved.

Tamsin popped the panel and rewired the lock in three quick motions. The door hissed open to reveal a narrow shaft sloping deeper into the planet.

"This leads to the pilgrim catacombs," the courier said, surprised despite himself.

"Then it's a better grave than the street," Tamsin said. She started down, then paused. "Name?" 

He hesitated. "Keir." 

"Keir-the-former-courier," she said. "If we live, you can decide whether to arrest me." 

They descended into cool darkness lit by bioluminescent moss. The singing from above faded to a distant ache. Keir clutched his wristcom, tapping frantic messages that returned only silence.

"They're jamming everything," he said. "Why would the Fleet jam its own couriers?"

Tamsin could have lied. It was often easier.

Instead, she handed him one of the stolen slivers.

"Because you weren't carrying messages," she said. "You were carrying secrets." 

Keir stared at the header glowing in the sliver's light.

"Ember Suns," he read. "I don't..." He looked at her. "What did you do?" 

"The same thing I'm about to do again," Tamsin said. "I'm going to steal this galaxy back one file at a time. The question is whether you want in." 

Keir laughed then, a short, broken sound.

"I wanted to be a historian," he said. "My family said couriers see history happen. I didn't think it would try to kill me." 

"History always tries to kill you," Tamsin said. "That's how it knows you were there." 

She turned deeper into the catacombs, the sliver's light painting the walls with the shape of a weapon designed to turn stars into graves.

Keir followed.

## Chapter 6 — The Senator's Flight

Maera Voss left the Senate through the waste reclamation corridors.

Pell Orun had chosen the route for its humiliation. "No one looks closely at what they have decided is beneath them," he had said, handing her a plain gray cloak and a data wafer containing the names of safe houses she had once voted to shut down.

As she walked, the vote results flashed across public displays.

**EMBER SUNS AUTHORIZATION ACT — PASSED**

Her name scrolled among the dissenters.

She expected sirens. Instead, there was only the low hum of pumps and the drip of condensation. The Protectorate would not arrest her publicly. They would wait. They would strip her committees, seize her accounts, freeze her travel, and then—when the outrage had been managed into boredom—remove her quietly.

She had no intention of waiting.

At junction thirteen, a maintenance lift awaited, its doors held open by a slender woman with a mechanic's tool belt and eyes too sharp to belong to any mechanic Maera had ever met.

"Senator Voss," the woman said. "I'm Nyx." 

"From Pell?" 

Nyx nodded. "He says you have a bad habit of trying to do the right thing at the worst possible time."

"He would be correct." 

They rode the lift down into levels the Senate pretended not to have. In the dark, Nyx handed Maera a compact blaster and a small disc of black crystal.

Maera turned it over in her palm. It seemed to drink the light.

"What's this?" 

"A resonance anchor," Nyx said. "Old Order tech. If we find the adept, they'll want to know you're not a trap. Carry it. It will feel... strange." 

It did. The crystal hummed faintly against her skin, like a remembered note.

They emerged into the undercity railworks and boarded a freight crawler bound for the orbital docks. Nyx sat across from her, legs braced, gaze scanning every car that passed.

"Where are we going?" Maera asked.

"Ashael," Nyx said. "There's a thief there with a talent for acquiring the wrong enemies. Pell thinks she can help us reach Thane's research lines." 

Maera thought of the image of the dead star. Of the Marshal's smile. Of the word *defensive* delivered like a prayer to power.

"Then let us find the thief," she said.

## Chapter 7 — The Ghost Ship *Lantern-Breaker*

Joren Kael stole the *Lantern-Breaker* on a dare from the dead.

Karth's northern wastes held the skeletons of the Schism Wars: half-buried cruisers, cracked walkers, and the rusting hulks of courier corvettes whose transponders still whispered long-finished arguments into empty skies. Master Ilyra had forbidden him from exploring them. After her death, prohibition felt like grief wearing a mask.

He found the corvette in a salt flat, its hull scarred but intact, its name barely visible beneath layers of dust.

*LANTERN-BREAKER.*

Inside, the air was stale, the consoles dark. Joren moved by the light of a wristlamp, fingers trailing across old control yokes and prayer tokens wedged into seams by crew long since turned to bone.

In the captain's chair, he found a sealed compartment.

Within it lay a navigation core and a single holotag etched with the Hollow Order's sigil.

Ilyra had been here.

The realization hit him like a physical blow. He sank into the chair, holotag in hand, and let himself feel the full weight of being the last student of a dead tradition on a dead world under a dead sky.

Then he slotted the nav core.

The ship woke with a cough of power. Systems flickered to life one by one: atmosphere recyclers, inertial dampers, a sensor array so old it rendered the horizon as a smear of static. The engine readouts blinked amber. Not good. Not hopeless.

On the auxiliary display, a log file auto-opened. A woman Joren recognized as Ilyra, younger and angrier, filled the screen.

"If you are seeing this," the recording said, "then Karth has fallen or I have. Possibly both. The *Lantern-Breaker* is yours now, though she won't make it far without a proper tune. The nav core holds a map to a place the Protectorate does not know exists. You will be tempted to go there immediately. Do not."

Joren snorted despite himself.

Ilyra's recorded gaze seemed to sharpen.

"First, you must learn the difference between surviving and serving," she said. "Survival keeps you breathing. Service gives the breath meaning. Find the others. The galaxy is about to need fools with convictions." 

The log ended.

Joren sat in the humming dark and wiped his eyes with the heel of his hand, annoyed to find tears there. He powered the engines. The corvette shuddered, sand cascading from its hull like an avalanche in reverse.

Above the salt flats, Protectorate patrols cut orderly patterns through the sky.

Joren keyed in a launch vector that would take him straight through them.

"This is either service or suicide," he muttered, and lifted.

## Chapter 8 — Convergences

Ashael burned in colors the eye had no name for.

The Protectorate's incursion had not been a bombardment but a harvesting. Massive lattice ships hung in the upper atmosphere, projecting shimmering nets of energy down onto the Singing Spires. Where the nets touched crystal, the singing changed pitch and poured upward in streams of light that vanished into the ships' bellies.

Tamsin watched from a catacomb vent with Keir beside her, both of them smeared in dust and bioluminescent spores. On her reader, the stolen files unfolded into a horrifying clarity: the Ember Suns required resonance-rich crystalline matrices to focus their suppression fields across interstellar distances.

Ashael was not being conquered.

It was being mined for the death of stars.

"We have to get this out," Keir said, voice raw. "People need to know." 

"People won't believe it," Tamsin said. "Not yet." 

"Then who?"

Tamsin thought of old rumor-nets and half-remembered contacts. Of a rebel cell she'd sold information to once and never heard from again. Of the way the word *Ember* seemed to ignite panic in every buried thread she tugged.

She opened her long-range spike transmitter, jury-rigged from courier parts and shrine relays. It would burn out after one burst. She had one shot.

She typed a message no sane person would send to strangers.

**HAVE PROOF EMBER SUNS ARE WEAPON. ASHAEL HARVEST IN PROGRESS. NEED EXTRACTION + DISTRIBUTION.**

She hesitated, then added:

**CAN PAY IN SECRETS.**

She fired the burst into the storm of jamming signals and prayed to no one.

---

Maera's stolen shuttle dropped below Ashael's cloud line in a sheath of static. Nyx cursed at the controls, one hand on the yoke, the other constantly adjusting power to keep them from being tagged by the lattice ships' sweeps.

"We'll have one landing window," Nyx said. "After that, we're a glowing confession." 

Maera gripped the resonance anchor in her pocket and felt its quiet hum answer the planet below.

"Find me the thief," she said.

---

Joren's corvette emerged from hyperspace too close to Ashael's moon and nearly tore itself apart correcting the vector. Alarms screamed. The *Lantern-Breaker* spun, then steadied, engines groaning like wounded animals.

His stolen nav core had guided him here, to a world he had never seen, because the map inside it pulsed in time with the Resonance like a heartbeat.

He had no plan beyond *arrive.*

On his sensor scopes, Ashael blazed with interference. Protectorate signatures crowded the bands. Beneath them, faint but persistent, was a narrowbeam burst repeating on a loop like a lighthouse in a storm.

**HAVE PROOF... NEED EXTRACTION...**

Joren exhaled slowly.

"Find the others," Ilyra had said.

He angled the *Lantern-Breaker* toward the signal.

## Chapter 9 — The Catacomb Accord

They met in a chamber carved from crystal that glowed with the soft light of trapped starlight.

Tamsin arrived first, blaster drawn, Keir at her shoulder. The chamber's walls hummed faintly, resonating with the distant harvesting above. It felt like standing inside a throat that had forgotten how to sing.

Nyx and Maera descended through a maintenance hatch moments later. Nyx's weapon was already in her hand. Maera's was not, but the way she held herself made Tamsin wary anyway.

"Senator," Tamsin said, recognizing her from a thousand newsfeeds. "You are either very brave or very stupid." 

"Both," Maera said. "You must be the thief." 

"Allegedly." Tamsin nodded at Nyx. "And you are the part where this becomes an arrest." 

Nyx snorted. "If I was here to arrest you, you'd already be cuffed." 

The air shifted.

All three women turned as a fifth figure stepped from the shadows: a young man in desert-worn clothes, carrying a staff of polished iron and looking as surprised as any of them to be in the room.

Joren Kael felt the resonance anchor in Maera's pocket like a bell rung underwater. He stared at her, at the crystal glow, at the thief with a blaster pointed at his chest.

"I got your signal," he said, to no one in particular.

"I didn't send a signal to *you*," Tamsin said.

"Does it matter?" Maera asked. "We are all here now." 

Keir, who had been silent, stepped forward and held up the data slivers like offerings.

"The Protectorate is killing stars," he said. "We can prove it." 

Maera met his gaze and saw not a courier but a witness.

"Then we do more than prove it," she said. "We stop it." 

Tamsin laughed, sharp and unbelieving.

"With what? A senator, a mechanic, a runaway monk, a courier, and a thief?" 

Joren's grief flared into something steadier.

"My master said the galaxy would need fools with convictions," he said. "I think this is what she meant." 

Above them, the harvesting nets pulsed brighter. Ashael's song climbed toward a breaking point.

Nyx tapped her wristcom, listening to the chatter of patrols converging on their sector.

"We can argue later," she said. "Right now we need to leave. Senator, you wanted someone who could break a weapon? Maybe this is him." She jerked her chin at Joren. "Maybe it's all of us." 

Tamsin lowered her blaster by a fraction.

"I have routes," she said. "And secrets." 

Maera drew the resonance anchor and held it out. The black crystal caught the chamber's light and drank it.

"I have access," she said. "And enemies." 

Joren placed his staff across his palms, the gesture instinctive and ceremonial.

"I have the Resonance," he said. "And a ship that might explode if we look at it wrong." 

Keir swallowed, then squared his shoulders.

"I have the courier codes," he said. "And a really strong desire not to die for nothing." 

Nyx smiled, wolfish and bright.

"Then we have a rebellion," she said.

## Chapter 10 — Embers in the Dark

They fled Ashael in pieces.

Nyx guided Maera through the undercity to a hidden hangar where smugglers once refueled. Tamsin and Keir triggered diversions along the catacomb routes, collapsing tunnels and looping surveillance feeds. Joren lifted the *Lantern-Breaker* through a storm of anti-air fire and brought her down hard enough to shear a landing strut, then held the ship together with equal parts desperation and prayer while the others sprinted up the ramp.

Protectorate fighters screamed overhead as the corvette clawed for orbit.

"We're too heavy," Nyx shouted from the co-pilot seat, wrestling with a console that seemed actively offended by being touched. "She won't clear the lattice net." 

Joren closed his eyes and reached for the Resonance.

It answered, bright and chaotic in the planet's crystalline well. He felt the net above them as a mesh of intent and energy, a pattern designed by minds that believed control was the same as safety.

He pushed.

For a breathless second, the net parted.

The *Lantern-Breaker* punched through into open space, engines screaming. Behind them, Ashael's song shattered into silence.

No one spoke for a long time.

Tamsin finally broke it, voice small in the hum of the ship.

"Where now?" 

Joren slotted Ilyra's nav core into the main console. A map bloomed across the display: a spiral arm of the galaxy marked by ember-red points, each one a star system recently dimmed or under threat. At the edge of the map, far beyond the Protectorate's usual patrol lanes, a single coordinate pulsed like a heartbeat.

**THE EXILE VAULT**

Maera leaned in, reading the annotation in an ancient script Pell had once taught her to recognize.

"A repository," she said. "A refuge."

"Or a trap," Tamsin said.

Joren thought of Ilyra's message. *You will be tempted to go there immediately. Do not.*

He set a course anyway.

"Then we walk into the trap together," he said.

Outside the viewport, the stars burned on—some bright, some dimming, some already gone.

In the shadow between them, a small ship with a broken strut and a reckless crew turned toward the unknown.

The war for the suns had begun.

## Epilogue — The Commander and the Ash

Lord-Commander Serix Thane watched the recording of Ashael's harvest in perfect silence.

He stood aboard the flagship *Radiant Verdict*, hands clasped behind his back, while the lattice ships' reports scrolled across the holotable in neat columns of success. Yield percentages. Resonance density. Projected suppression range increases.

And, flagged in red:

**UNAUTHORIZED EXTRACTION — DATA THEFT LIKELY — CORVETTE ESCAPE CONFIRMED**

A lieutenant waited, rigid with fear.

"We tracked the corvette's exit vector, my lord," she said. "It matches an antiquated Hollow transponder signature. We believe—"

"You believe a ghost stole from me," Thane said, not turning.

"Yes, my lord." 

He considered the word *ghost*.

In his youth, he had studied under Republic admirals who treated war as geometry and mercy as a rounding error. He had learned from them that the only lasting peace was the one enforced so thoroughly no one remembered alternatives.

The Ember Suns were not cruelty. They were arithmetic.

Still, ghosts had a way of unbalancing equations.

"Issue a sector-wide directive," he said at last. "Priority gamma. Authorize the Inquisitorium. I want the adept alive. The thief... optional. The senator... broken." 

"Yes, my lord." 

When the lieutenant had gone, Thane reached out and dimmed the holotable projection of Ashael's crystalline glow until it was no more than a fading ember in the dark.

"Run," he said softly to the empty room. "There is nowhere you can run that I cannot reach."

In the silent gulf between stars, his weapon waited to be fed.
